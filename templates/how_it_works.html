How our models work

Every prediction on this site comes from box scores and play-by-play, turned into team ratings and fed to models that were tested on seasons they never saw. Here's what goes in, what comes out, and how often it's right.

1. Elo: a rating for every team

Every team starts at 1500. After each game, the winner takes rating points from the loser: more for an upset, more for a lopsided score. Between seasons every rating slides part of the way back toward average, because rosters turn over.

2. EPA: how efficient each play was

A 6-yard gain on 3rd-and-5 is worth more than 6 yards on 3rd-and-10. Expected points added (EPA) captures that: every down, distance and field position has an expected number of points for the offense, and a play's EPA is how much it raised or lowered that expectation. Success rate is the share of plays with positive EPA.

Raw averages flatter teams with weak schedules, so we fit every play in a season at once with a ridge regression that credits each offense and defense for its opponent. The result is opponent-adjusted EPA per play, the offense and defense numbers you'll see on team pages. Quarterbacks get their own rating from the same data, so a starter's injury moves the forecast.

3. The prediction models

Before each game we build a few hundred features: both teams' Elo, recent and season scoring margin, adjusted EPA and success rate, quarterback ratings, rest, travel, weather and, in college, recruiting and roster talent. Three models turn those into a win probability, a margin and a total:

  • Linear, a regression on about a dozen strong features. It's our pick for the NFL, where there isn't enough data to trust anything fancier.
  • XGBoost, gradient-boosted trees on every feature. It's our pick for college, where 130+ teams and a much wider range of talent give it room to find interactions.
  • Market-adjusted starts from the closing betting line and learns small corrections. It's the most accurate, but it isn't independent of Vegas.

Neither independent model sees a betting line, so when they disagree with Vegas, that's a genuine difference of opinion. Those disagreements are what we feature on the league pages.

4. Preseason ratings

Before a snap is played, each team gets a rating in points versus an average team, built from five sources: the last two seasons of efficiency, incoming talent (recruiting in college, the draft in the NFL), returning production, player movement (the transfer portal, free agency and trades) and coaching changes. For college we also estimate each team's chance of a top-10 season, using what past elite teams had in common.

Vegas is better than we are, and so far no model on this site beats the closing line against the spread. Our models are close to the line, not ahead of it. They're useful for understanding games, not for betting.
